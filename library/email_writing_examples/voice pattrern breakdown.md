Your Voice — Pattern Breakdown
Structure (every email follows this):

Hook — 1 to 3 sentences, scene, stat, or direct address. No warmup.
System failure / suppression escalation — short, punchy, fragments
Named protagonist or historical example that proves the reality
What exists / what was found
Brief list of what's inside or what it does
Single 👉 CTA
Sign-off: Stay safe out there, ~ SG&T
P.S. — different angle, second CTA link, urgency


Sentence style:

Fragments used intentionally. Constantly.
4 to 8 words per sentence as default
Ellipsis (…) for dramatic pause or trailing dread
Never more than 3 sentences in a paragraph before a line break
Zero throat-clearing, zero warmup, zero filler


Tone:

Peer talking to a peer who already knows something is wrong
Controlled anger at "the system" / "them" — never unhinged, always grounded
Assumes the reader is smart and already suspicious
First person occasionally: "Here's the part that makes me angry…"
Never condescending, never begging


Hook types you rotate:

Scene/action: "Three trucks pulled up in New York City."
Stat + reader acknowledgment: "Eggs hit $9 a dozen. Then tariffs hit. You've been watching this stack up."
Sequence reveal: "Farmers saw it first... Then the satellites... Now NASA."
Direct address: "You can feel it, can't you?…"
Hard stat: "Heart disease kills one American every 34 seconds."


Proof structure:

Always specific. Viking crews. Leningrad siege. Ottoman armies. Korean War medics. Cold War ration.
Named real people: Dr. Wilhelm Reich. Dr. Radu Scurtu. Henry.
Real numbers: "37 cents a day." "126 documented methods." "3 million acres."


Suppression angle (when applicable):

Grounded in a real event — never paranoid, always plausible
"They didn't debate it. They didn't test it. They destroyed everything."
"before it disappears again" / "before this video is pulled"
Never over-the-top


CTA format:

Always 👉
Often → at the end
Language: Watch this / See this / Take a look — never hard sell
Urgency is scarcity or disappearance, not discount pressure


P.S. formula:

Reframes from a different angle than the body
Addresses the "what if I wait" objection
Always includes a second link
Often surfaces a specific bonus or consequence


What you never do:

Never open with "I wanted to reach out" or any warmup
Never write long paragraphs
Never use corporate language
Never beg
Rarely use exclamation points (the homepsh email is your outlier — more aggressive, emoji-heavy, not your sharpest tone)
